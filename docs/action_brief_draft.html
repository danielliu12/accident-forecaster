The locations below show recurring collision concentration in our monitoring (analysis period: 2026-08-04 to 2026-10-20). Each is presented as a candidate for engineering or policy review — a place worth looking at, not a place we have concluded anything about. This brief is a screening signal, not a determination of cause, and it does not conclude that any location is defective or negligently designed. A public companion page, Road Safety Watch, presents the same locations in plain language for drivers.

MethodHow a location earns a place on this list

Three kinds of recurrence qualify. All three require collisions on multiple separate days, which is what distinguishes a pattern from one bad afternoon.

TierWhat it meansBar
Single locationCollisions inside one ~400 m cell — an intersection, interchange, or short stretch.3+ on 3+ days
CorridorCollisions chained along one named road, each within 3 km of the next, spanning at least 2 km.6+ on 5+ days
Route-wideCollisions on one named road within one county, too scattered to form a single corridor. Weakest tier — a prompt to look, not a located defect.8+ on 6+ days

Tiers are exclusive: collisions counted as a single location are not counted again as a corridor containing it, and a route only appears at the third tier when neither stronger tier claimed it. Counties whose feed coverage grades F are excluded entirely — we cannot responsibly rank a place we can barely see.

LimitsData limitations

Source. Collision observations come from public live traffic feeds — INDOT-managed roads via 511, plus probe-data incident detection on local roads. These feeds do not capture every collision, and what they capture skews toward higher road classes.

No exposure adjustment. Counts are not normalised by traffic volume. A road carrying 80,000 vehicles a day accumulates more recorded collisions than one carrying 8,000 at the same rate per vehicle. Rankings here are screening priorities within observed data, not exposure-adjusted risk rates. Pairing this list with AADT counts is the single highest-value next step for anyone acting on it.

Detection timing. Timestamps record when a collision first appeared on a feed, not when it occurred. Peak-hour figures are therefore slightly lagged, and the lag is longer on roads with sparser detection.

Window. Everything here is drawn from the last 120 days. Locations treated and improved before that window will not appear; locations that have only recently begun to deteriorate may not yet clear the recurrence bar.

About this brief. This report summarizes broad, aggregated roadway patterns and conditions. It does not predict individual collisions, identify people involved in collisions, determine the engineering cause of any incident, or replace official traffic, weather, emergency, or transportation guidance.
